      <p>The shape of a content team in 2026 is not the shape it was
      in 2016. Marketing doesn't live in the CMS anymore. Briefs draft
      in <strong>Google Docs</strong>. Project plans run in
      <strong>Monday</strong> or <strong>Airtable</strong>. Brand
      assets ship out of <strong>Adobe Express</strong> or
      <strong>Canva</strong>. Internal docs sit in
      <strong>Office365</strong> and <strong>Sharepoint</strong>. Each
      team picks the tool that fits the way they actually work, and
      the website is just one surface among many that has to keep up.</p>

      <p>That's a shift toward loosely-coupled systems — and it's a
      good shift. The old model, where one CMS owned everything from
      the editorial calendar to the asset library to the publishing
      surface, never really fit how teams operate. The new model says:
      let the marketing team work in the tools they're already
      productive in, and design the website so it can pull from any of
      them.</p>

      <p>Pantheon's newer offerings make that practical at the
      hosting layer — first-class support for headless and decoupled
      architectures, the Next.js platform, and edge delivery that
      handles the speed and scale problems before they reach a single
      line of application code. Combined with our decade-plus of
      WebOps experience, that's the leverage we're aiming at: a site
      that meets each team where they already work, instead of asking
      them to come back to the CMS.</p>

      <p>The work falls into three shapes:</p>

      <ul>
        <li><strong>New site design and build.</strong> Strategy,
        design, and engineering for a Drupal, WordPress, or Next.js
        site standing up on Pantheon from scratch. The output is the
        site, the WebOps workflow around it, and a team that knows
        how to run both.</li>
        <li><strong>Site migration.</strong> Whether that's moving a
        corporate site onto Pantheon from another host, or moving
        between Pantheon platforms as the right shelf changes. We run
        migrations against the actual content, not a sample, with
        re-runnable scripts that survive the inevitable late-stage
        changes.</li>
        <li><strong>Site replatform.</strong> A more substantial
        rebuild that swaps the underlying CMS or framework — Drupal
        to WordPress, WordPress to Next.js, monolith to decoupled.
        These are the projects where the platform shape and the
        editorial workflow have drifted apart, and the answer isn't a
        refresh.</li>
      </ul>
