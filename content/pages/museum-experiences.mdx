    title: The Challenge
    background: white
    richText: >
      <p>Visitors arrive with phones in hand and an expectation that the
      exhibit will meet them halfway. They want to touch, scrub, replay,
      and compare. They want to leave with the story, not a list of
      dates.</p>

      <p>The pressure on curators and exhibit designers is to translate
      dense scholarship into something a family of four can navigate in
      twelve minutes — without flattening it. The pressure on the
      institution's IT team is to deliver software that holds up to ten
      thousand fingerprints a day and never freezes during a school
      visit.</p>
  - type: MainSection
    tagline: Story first, then the interaction model
    title: The Approach
    background: gray
    richText: >
      <p>Most of the work happens before any code is written. Curators,
      educators, and exhibit designers already know the story they want
      visitors to leave with. Our job is to find the interaction model
      that delivers it.</p>

      <p>We sit in on planning meetings, sketch user flows on paper, and
      gray-box the experience in 3D before anyone commits to hardware.
      Spatial planning, sensor placement, and accessibility get worked
      out alongside the narrative, not bolted on later.</p>

      <p>Once the shape is right, production starts: real-time 3D,
      custom integrations with whatever the gallery needs (RFID readers,
      gesture sensors, projection mapping, audio triggers), and the
      unglamorous work that nobody sees but everybody notices when it's
      missing — the wiring, the kiosk enclosures, the stress testing in
      the actual room with actual lighting.</p>

      <p>We stay through opening day, train the museum's staff to
      operate and troubleshoot the exhibit, and provide ongoing support
      so it still works on the show's tenth anniversary.</p>
  - type: MainSection
    tagline: Why Unity, when it fits
    title: The Tools
    background: white
    richText: >
      <p><strong>Unity</strong> is the engine we reach for when an
      exhibit needs photorealistic rendering, real-time physics, or an
      interaction model that doesn't fit a browser. It also lets us
      build once and deploy across touchscreens, projections, AR
      headsets, and mobile companion apps from the same codebase —
      useful when a single exhibit has to live on three different
      surfaces.</p>

      <p>It's not always the right tool. For a content-heavy interactive
      that mostly needs to read like a beautiful book, a web stack is
      faster to build and cheaper to maintain over the life of the
      exhibit. We'll tell you when that's the case.</p>
