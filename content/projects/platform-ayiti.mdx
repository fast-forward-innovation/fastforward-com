      <p>We built Platform Ayiti as a teaching library, not just a publishing
      site. Teachers can do more than find materials. They curate their
      favorite sources into lists and share those lists with other teachers.
      We wanted it to be really, really fast on the connections teachers
      actually have. Pages load quickly over mobile data, and anything a
      teacher saves stays on their phone, so it still works when the
      connection drops.</p>

      <p>The heart of the new platform is Asistan (Kreyòl for "assistant").
      A teacher asks for what they need in plain words. For example: "Do you
      have materials for teaching grade 4 science about water conservation?"
      Asistan answers with a list of matching resources from the library, and
      the teacher can save them straight into a list.</p>

      <p>Teachers can ask in Haitian Creole, French, English or any of about
      140 other languages. Asistan works out the language of the question and
      answers in the same language. That supports teachers who want to work in
      Haitian Creole, and it welcomes French and English speakers too. When
      nothing in the library matches, Asistan asks follow-up questions and
      suggests other resources that could help.</p>

      <p><strong>Asistan stays inside the library.</strong> It only answers
      about content on the site, or content the site links to or references.
      It does not generate teaching materials, and it never recommends
      anything outside the library. Every answer points a teacher to real
      material made by other teachers.</p>

      <p>Every page on Platform Ayiti is built ahead of time with
      <strong>Next.js</strong> and served from a CDN close to the reader. The
      server does the heavy lifting, so phones download as little JavaScript
      as possible. That is what keeps pages quick over mobile data.</p>

      <p>The people who run the site edit it in <strong>Pantheon P1</strong>.
      It holds the content and media, and gives editors a visual page editor
      built on the open-source Puck editor and Tiptap. Editors see the page
      they are building as they build it.</p>

      <p>Teacher accounts live in <strong>Google Cloud</strong>, using
      Firebase for sign-in and identity. Each teacher's preferences and saved
      lists are stored there too, and cached on their device so the lists
      still work offline. Teachers reorder lists with up and down arrows
      instead of dragging, so keyboard and screen-reader users can manage them
      as well.</p>

      <p>Asistan runs on <strong>Google Vertex AI</strong>. We index all of
      the site's content and give it to the model as context. That is what
      keeps its answers inside the library. Search works the same way on a
      smaller scale: a compact index filtered in the browser, with every
      result a page search engines can crawl.</p>

      <p>We also used AI in our own process. It helped us audit the content
      structure to shape the content model, and it helped us build repeatable
      Playwright test suites.</p>

    tagline: Fast on any connection, useful even without one

    richText: >
      <p>Most teachers reach Platform Ayiti on a phone over mobile data, so we
      tested it the way they use it. We compared four pages on the old site
      and the new one with Google Lighthouse, under a simulated Haitian mobile
      connection of 7 Mbps with 100 ms of latency. Every page got faster. The
      home page now shows its first content in 0.73 seconds, down from 1.59,
      and the server starts responding in 0.07 seconds instead of nearly a
      full second. Pages are lighter too. The home page dropped from 2.5 MB to
      1.0 MB, and the blog index went from 6.1 MB to under 1 MB. On mobile
      data, every megabyte a teacher doesn't download counts.</p>

      <p>The rebuild also made the library easier to find. Every page we
      tested now scores 100 for SEO in Lighthouse, up from the low to mid
      80s, and accessibility scores rose on every page.</p>

      <p>Speed only helps when there is a connection, and in Haiti that isn't
      a given. Platform Ayiti is a progressive web application, so it keeps
      working when the signal drops. A teacher's saved materials and lists
      are stored on their device, so they can open them in the classroom
      with limited or no internet access.</p>

      <p>The platform does more for teachers than it used to. Asistan answers
      in the teacher's own language and points them to materials that fit
      their lesson. Teachers save what they find into lists, then share those
      lists with colleagues, so a good unit plan can travel from one
      classroom to the next.</p>
